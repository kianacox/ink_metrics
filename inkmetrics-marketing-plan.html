Core message focus
Lead with take-home clarity, automatic chair rent maths, and the action cards that surface clients going quiet. These are live, provable, and genuinely differentiated.

Messaging

The three angles that will convert

Every piece of content — organic, paid, or community — should map to one of these. Rotate between them to avoid repetition while staying on-brand.

Pillar 1 · Pain
"Do you actually know your real take-home after chair rent?"
Targets the gut feeling most artists have — that bookings look full but the money doesn't add up once chair rent and supplies come out. No solution mentioned. Just the question.
Pillar 2 · Aspiration
"Log a session in 30 seconds. See your take-home update live."
Shows what clarity feels like. Use real screenshots of the take-home summary, retention rate, or action cards. Authenticity over polish — this audience spots mockups immediately.
Pillar 3 · Trust
"Built by a developer whose partner is a working tattoo artist."
The strongest long-term differentiator. The founder story is genuine and resonates hard with a community that's suspicious of tech built by people who don't understand their world.

What to avoid: Don't lead with features ("dashboard", "analytics", "export"). Lead with the outcome — "know your real take-home", "spot the regulars going quiet", "see the money sitting on the table". Features are proof points, not hooks. And use artist language: take-home, chair rent, sessions, regulars, VIPs. Never "net income", "studio fee", "transactions", or "returning clients".

Hook bank

Ready-to-use copy that maps to real features

Each hook below is tied to a metric or feature that exists in the app today. Mix and match across Reels, ad creative, and email subject lines.

Take-home
"You billed £2,030 last month. Want to know what you actually took home?"
Specific number = scroll-stopper. Pair with the take-home summary screenshot. The reveal: £1,281 after chair rent and supplies.
Action cards
"There's £550 sitting on the table. ink_metrics just told me which client to message."
Pair with the In Progress card screenshot. The hook is the revenue-at-risk number — that's the wedge.
Client tiers
"58% of your clients come back. Do you know which 42% don't?"
Lifetime retention rate as the hook. Show the clients page with VIP and Regular tags. Identify-the-gap framing.
Profit per session
"Your day rate is £400. Your real profit per session is £256. Here's why."
Show the breakdown: chair rent and expenses eating into the headline number. The "ah" moment for any artist who's never done this maths.
Expenses
"You spent £241 on supplies last month. Most of it wasn't ink."
Counterintuitive reveal — travel and equipment usually outweigh ink spend. Pair with the Expenses by category screenshot.
Founder
"My partner's a tattoo artist. I built this so she'd stop dreading her tax return."
The trust pillar in one sentence. Use as the pinned launch Reel and the about page lead.

Deferred

What's not in scope yet

MTD / compliance angle
Deferred until data export is built and all compliance claims are verified. Don't market what you can't fully deliver yet.
SEO content hub
Worth building from month 2 onwards — a definitive "tattoo artist take-home UK" guide will compound over time. Not a pre-launch priority.

Search intent for this category is too low-volume to be efficient at £200/mo. Revisit once organic SEO data shows which terms actually convert.
Convention presence
High impact but requires time and travel. Revisit at 6 months once there's traction to point to and a story to tell in person.
