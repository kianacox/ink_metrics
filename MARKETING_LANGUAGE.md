# ink_metrics — Marketing Language Guide

Shared vocabulary for marketing copy, campaigns, social, and agent prompts. Defines how to talk about the product, what terms artists use, and what to avoid.

---

## Who We're Talking To

**The artist.** An independent tattoo artist who runs their own book — likely solo or renting a chair in a studio. They are not accountants. They want to know if their business is healthy without drowning in spreadsheets. They probably already use booking software (Fresha, Booksy, Tattoodo, etc.) and we do not replace that.

**Core tension to acknowledge in copy:** artists are creative professionals who hate admin. ink_metrics removes the admin, not the artistry.

---

## Product Description

**ink_metrics** is a business health app for tattoo artists. It gives artists financial visibility and client insights without forcing them to change how they book or work.

**What it is not:** a booking tool, a POS system, a CRM, an accounting platform.

**Positioning:** the simplest way for a tattoo artist to know if their business is actually working.

## Core Concepts — How to Talk About Them

### Session

**What it is:** A tattoo job. One sitting, one client, one price.

**How artists say it:** "a job", "a tattoo", "an appointment", "a booking"

**How we say it in copy:** session, job, appointment (all acceptable). "Session" is our product term but doesn't need to be forced into every sentence.

**Do not say:** "transaction", "service", "event"

---

### Client

**What it is:** A person the artist has tattooed or is tracking.

**How artists say it:** "my client", "my regular", "my customer"

**How we say it in copy:** client (preferred). "customer" is acceptable in broader contexts.

---

### Chair Rent

**What it is:** What the artist pays their studio to work there. Either a flat weekly/monthly fee or a percentage of earnings (commission).

**How artists say it:** "chair rent", "studio rent", "my cut to the studio", "percentage"

**How we say it in copy:** chair rent (always). This is the artist's native term — use it.

**Do not say:** "studio fee", "compensation arrangement", "paid to studio"

**Why it matters in copy:** Chair rent is often an artist's biggest hidden cost. Most artists don't know their real take-home after chair rent. That's the problem we solve.

---

### Take-Home

**What it is:** What the artist actually earns after chair rent and expenses. Gross revenue minus all deductions.

**How artists say it:** "what I actually make", "take-home", "my cut", "what hits my account"

**How we say it in copy:** take-home, take-home pay, what you actually earn

**Do not say:** "net income", "profit margin", "net revenue"

---

### Gross

**What it is:** Total revenue from sessions before any deductions.

**How we say it in copy:** "total earned", "gross revenue", "what clients paid you" — avoid the raw term "gross" in consumer copy; it's jargon. Use in charts/labels only.

---

### Expenses

**What it is:** Business costs the artist logs: ink, needles, cartridges, machines, travel, etc.

**How artists say it:** "my costs", "supplies", "kit", "overheads"

**How we say it in copy:** expenses, costs, supplies. Specific categories (ink, needles) are more vivid than the generic word.

**Expense categories (use these exact labels):**

- Ink
- Cartridges
- Needles
- Machines
- Other equipment
- Travel
- Miscellaneous

---

### Client Tiers

**What it is:** ink_metrics automatically classifies every client into one of three tiers based on how many times they've been tattooed.


| Tier          | What it means                 | Threshold    |
| ------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------ |
| **One-timer** | Came once, hasn't returned    | 0–1 sessions |
| **Regular**   | Coming back, building loyalty | 2–4 sessions |
| **VIP**       | Core loyal client             | 5+ sessions  |


**How to talk about tiers in copy:**

- "See which clients are regulars and which are slipping away."
- "Know your VIPs at a glance."
- "Find out how many one-timers came back."

**Do not say:** "returning client" (binary old label, replaced by tiers), "customer segment", "cohort"

---

### Action Cards (Retention Nudges)

**What it is:** ink_metrics spots clients who may need a follow-up and tells the artist. Three types:


| Card            | What triggers it                                           | What to say in copy                        |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Retention**   | A regular hasn't come back in 8+ weeks                     | "Your regulars going quiet"                |
| **Follow-up**   | A first-timer hasn't returned in 6+ weeks                  | "First-timers who haven't booked again"    |
| **In Progress** | A session was logged but not marked complete after 4 weeks | "Sessions you may have forgotten to close" |


**Revenue at risk:** Each card shows the estimated money at stake — the average session price for that client.

**How to talk about action cards in copy:**

- "ink_metrics tells you which clients to reach out to."
- "Never let a regular go quiet without knowing."
- "See the money sitting on the table."

**Do not say:** "AI-powered", "alerts", "notifications" (these are passive nudges in the dashboard, not push notifications)

---

### Monthly History / Analytics

**What it is:** Artists can look back month by month at their financial performance — earnings, chair rent, expenses, take-home, and client retention.

**How to talk about it in copy:**

- "See how last month stacked up."
- "Know which months were your best — and why."
- "Your financial history, at a glance."

**Do not say:** "historical analytics dashboard", "time-series data"

---

### Retention Rate

**What it is:** The percentage of clients who have come back for more than one session. Calculated across all time — not just the current month.

**How to talk about it in copy:**

- "How many clients come back?"
- "Your loyalty rate."
- "The number that tells you if clients love your work."

---

### Average Profit Per Session

**What it is:** Take-home divided by the number of sessions in a month. The real money made per job after chair rent and expenses.

**How to talk about it in copy:**

- "What you actually made per session."
- "Not the price — the profit."

---

## Tone and Voice Principles

- **Talk like an artist, not an accountant.** Use "take-home" not "net income". Use "chair rent" not "compensation".
- **Acknowledge the frustration.** Artists know they're bad at admin. Don't pretend it's easy — celebrate that ink_metrics makes it easier.
- **Respect the craft.** Never position the business side as more important than the art. The business exists to protect the art.
- **Be direct.** Artists are busy. Get to the point.
- **Avoid corporate language.** No "leverage", "synergy", "holistic", "streamline your workflow".
